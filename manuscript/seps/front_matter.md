\## Abstract



National education systems evolve through interactions among school access, adult educational attainment, youth literacy, and public education expenditure. However, it remains uncertain whether these aggregated state variables contain a reproducible signal for predicting subsequent educational change. This study develops and validates a regularized dynamic-system framework using open UNESCO Institute for Statistics data covering 222 countries and territories between 1970 and 2025. The primary state vector comprises the out-of-school rate at primary age, adult lower-secondary-or-higher attainment, youth literacy, and government education expenditure as a share of gross domestic product. Annualized changes were estimated using the observed time intervals, and predictive performance was evaluated against a zero-change baseline through country-grouped cross-validation, nested model selection, temporal-origin validation, leave-one-country-out analysis, cluster bootstrap, and temporal-window sensitivity tests. Only the adult educational-attainment equation consistently contained predictive information. Ridge regression reduced root mean squared error by 5.34% for consecutive annual transitions and by 6.73% for transitions of up to two years. In temporal-origin validation, Ridge outperformed zero change in four of five tests. Across 15 temporal-window tests, 12 were positive, including all nine tests based on intervals of up to two years. Coefficient signs were 80% consistent across temporal models, while prior adult attainment and youth literacy retained stable signs in every specification. Nonlinear machine-learning models did not provide a consistent advantage over regularized linear models. The findings indicate a small but reproducible predictive signal for changes in adult educational attainment, especially when moderate temporal gaps are permitted. The framework should be interpreted as a planning and monitoring tool rather than a causal model or high-confidence forecasting system.

\## Highlights



\- An open-data dynamic framework was evaluated across 222 countries and territories.

\- Adult educational attainment was the only consistently predictable state equation.

\- Ridge reduced RMSE by 5.34% annually and 6.73% with gaps of up to two years.

\- Temporal validation was positive in four of five tests and showed no future-data leakage.

\- All nine temporal tests with gaps of up to two years outperformed zero change.
